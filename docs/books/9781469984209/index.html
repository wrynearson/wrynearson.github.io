My Notes

This was a fun book, and explored some interesting topics. What's required for a society to thrive? Who benefits and suffers from a "functioning" society? Can a lie keep people safe?

These questions were explored via the plot of a closed-off, underground silo (144 floors, to be precise) that is self sufficient. Despite the (literal) depth, the characters were somewhat shallow and predictable, but the pacing and plot made up for it.

While I'm not immediately going to read the next books in the series, I hope to do so one day to continue to discover why they're in the silo, and what exists beyond its walls.
