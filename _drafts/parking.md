As the stereotype goes, things in ~~Texas~~ the US are bigger than elsewhere. So to is how towns and cities _feel_ when you're not in a two ton hunk of metal.

I'm not talking about the suburbs certainly are more spread out than cities dues to their terrible land usage – I'm referring to how big the distance feels between from the place you are to the place you want to go to.

...

## Bozeman

Bozeman is where I lived while at university. It has an historic center, neighborhoods, and even has a train line running through it (although only for cargo).

It is also extremely car brained, and doesn't even have a bus connection to and from the airport

### Parking Minimums

Bozeman has some [fairly standard parking minimums](https://library.municode.com/mt/bozeman/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH38UNDECO_ART5PRDE_DIV38.540PA_S38.540.050NUPASPRE) by US standards, especially for commercial use.

For example, hospitals require one parking space _per hospital bed_.

There are other hilarious and terrifying examples, such as for:

- car dealerships:
  > 1 space per 200 square feet of indoor floor area; plus 1 space per 20 outdoor vehicle display spaces
- bowling alleys:
  > 2 spaces per lane; plus 2 spaces per billiard table
- Restaurants, cafes, and **bars**:
  > 1 space per 50 square feet of indoor public serving area

In many cases, the amount of parking space required exceeds the amount of space being used by the business itself. The [US large standard parking space](https://www.dimensions.com/element/parking-spaces) is 16.7m2 (180ft2).

The legal code claims to "accommodate bicycle-riding residents", but at a much lower amount:

> The number of bicycle parking spaces must be at least **ten percent** of the number of automobile parking stalls required, despite taking up less than one tenth of the space (15ft2)

## Effect

These codes, present all over the US, are contrary to the goal of getting _people_ (not two ton hunks of metal) from point A to point B.

Businesses have to accommodate these rules, even though adding car parking reduces profit margins (_reference_). Sprawl also increases municipal spending, reduces tax revenue, and a long list of other issues.
