# Summary

It's not about laziness or freedom, it's about boring urban planning policy which makes it almost impossible to live without a car, mainly due to urban spawl (single family detatched home zoning laws) and a lack of non-car infrastructure investment. Needing a car makes communities worse. It impacts all of us, but affects the marginalized the most. We can change it.

# Outline

- Intro

- Sustainability statistics
  - Transportation sector is #2 emitter (16%) for GHG https://ourworldindata.org/emissions-by-sector 
    - ![Emissions by sector – pie charts](https://ourworldindata.org/uploads/2020/09/Emissions-by-sector-%E2%80%93-pie-charts.png)
    - ![per-capita-ghg-sector](/Users/will/Documents/GitHub/wrynearson.github.io/assets/img/urban-sustainability/per-capita-ghg-sector-usa.svg)
  - 11.9% is road transport, 1.9% aviation
  - 60% of road transport (7.1% total) is passenger travel (cars, busses, taxis and motorcycles)
  - **If we want to be more sustainable, we need to reduce emission in this sector.**
  
- Ways to reduce transportation CO2 emissions
  - Electric cars for everyone
    - Better than petrol/diesel, but expensive, and pointless if the grid is fossil fuel driven
  - Public transportation
    - More efficient (cost and GHG) than individual car ownership
  
- Car ownership problems
  - For the driver
    - Expensive, including unexpected "shocks" (i.e., fixing a broken car)
    - Stressful
  - For the community
    - Dangerous (many driver-to-driver and driver-pedestrian injuries and deaths)
    - Less livable and enjoyable
      - Polluting (air, noise)
      - Hotter (heat island from asphault)
      - Spread out
    - Environmental impacts
    - Health impacts
      - From above, also less movement due to driving.
  
- **It wasn't always like this, and doesn't have to be like this**

- Urban sprawl

  - > It is illegal on 75 percent of the residential land in many American cities to build anything other than a detached single-family home. https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2019/06/18/upshot/cities-across-america-question-single-family-zoning.html?action=click&module=Top%20Stories&pgtype=Homepage

- How to change it
  - Tell your politicians to prioritize public transit, bike infrastructure, sidewalks and other non-car focused infrastructure.
  - Tell your friends that things can change and be better.
  - **The main reason Americans want to own a car is because they (we) think we need one. With current policy and urban planning, we often do.**
