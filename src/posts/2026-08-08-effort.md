*I’m not so certain of this thesis, but want to share it nonetheless*.

Technology often promises to make our lives easier by maintaining or increasing output while decreasing input. Washing machines decrease the time and effort to wash clothes than a washboard. A train let's us go further in an hour than our legs.

Computers can do math faster than human computers. The internet has more information than our local library.

These promises mostly panned out. But they didn't (dramatically) make us less busy – they raised expectations. Clothes are clean, we can travel, and calculations are instant. But we no longer have wash days (well, Switzerland does), I'm writing this from a train, and our modern lives depend on more calculations, not to mention all of the other things we now have time to do.

The world became more `efficient` (good) but also more `producvtive` (also good, to an extent).

LLMs are the next big promise. They answer our questions without judgement, can code a full app with a single prompt, and can find and synthesis vast amounts of information. 

Based on history, this technology should, and already is, fulfilling the same promise. It takes less effort to maintain or increase output. But expectations will also rise.
